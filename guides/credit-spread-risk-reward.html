Credit spread risk, reward, and breakeven win rate

6 min read · Updated November 3, 2026

A credit spread pays you up front and caps your risk. The trade-off is that the risk is usually larger than the reward, so it’s worth knowing exactly what win rate your spreads need.

The basic math

A put credit spread sells a put and buys a lower-strike put in the same expiration. A call credit spread sells a call and buys a higher-strike call.

  • Max profit = credit received × 100
  • Max loss = (spread width − credit) × 100
  • Breakeven: short put strike − credit (put spread), or short call strike + credit (call spread)
Example: sell the 450 put and buy the 445 put for $1.25. The width is 5, so max profit is $125, max loss is (5 − 1.25) × 100 = $375, and breakeven is 448.75.

The win rate a spread needs

If every trade ended at max profit or max loss, the breakeven win rate would be max loss ÷ (max profit + max loss). For a 5-wide spread:

CreditMax profitMax lossBreakeven win rate
$0.50$50$45090%
$1.00$100$40080%
$1.25$125$37575%
$1.67$167$33367%
$2.50$250$25050%

Real trades rarely end exactly at the maximums, because you can close early. That’s why your actual average win and loss, from your journal, matter more than the theoretical ones.

What to track for your spreads

  • Credit as a percentage of width, for every trade
  • How often you take a full or near-full loss
  • Your average win and average loss when you close early
  • Results by distance from the money and by days to expiration
