How to set trading rules you can actually measure

5 min read · Updated November 3, 2026

“Be more disciplined” isn’t a rule. A good trading rule is specific enough that you can look at any trade afterward and say, without arguing with yourself, whether you followed it.

The test for a good rule

A measurable rule has three parts:

  1. A clear condition: a time, a number, or an event
  2. A clear action: what you do or don’t do
  3. A yes-or-no check: you can tell from your trade record whether you followed it

Turning vague rules into measurable ones

VagueMeasurable
Don’t overtradeNo more than 3 new trades per day
Don’t chase the openNo new trades before 10:00 ET
Cut lossesClose any trade once it loses $150
Don’t revenge tradeWait 60 minutes after a losing trade
Keep size reasonableNo more than 2 contracts per trade
Respect news daysNo new trades on Fed announcement days

How to track them

  • Start with one or two rules. Too many at once makes it hard to tell what’s working.
  • Count from the day you start. Judge a rule by trades made after you adopted it, not by your past.
  • Compare results. Look at your average result on trades that followed the rule versus trades that broke it.
  • Tag the ones you can’t check automatically. For a rule like “no trading on Fed days,” tag the trades where you broke it.

Review them monthly

Once a month, ask three questions for each rule: How often did I follow it? What did breaking it cost? Is it still the right rule? Keep the ones that clearly help, and adjust or drop the ones that don’t.
